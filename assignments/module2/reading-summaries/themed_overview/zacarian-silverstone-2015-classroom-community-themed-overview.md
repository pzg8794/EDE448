## Central Argument

Classroom community begins before students enter the room. Zacarian and Silverstone follow a teacher who studies the incoming class, prepares the physical environment, learns about strengths and access needs, consults records and colleagues, and builds communication with families. This preparation is framed as important as lesson planning because it creates trust and prevents avoidable problems.

## Questions the Teacher Asks

The chapter encourages proactive inquiry about:

- IEPs and required accommodations
- amplification, visual support, seating, mobility, and workspace needs
- sensory, movement, rest, and therapeutic needs
- home languages and family communication
- English-language development and interrupted schooling
- poverty, housing instability, food, clothing, and other material conditions
- prior conflicts and social-emotional supports
- staff and specialists who share responsibility
- student work, strengths, interests, names, and established relationships

These questions are not a license to collect private information without purpose. They are a reminder that classroom design should respond to the actual community rather than an imagined default learner.

## Prevention Through Preparation

The source broadens behavior support beyond an intervention used after difficulty. A teacher can:

- arrange the room for access before the first day
- communicate with families in languages they understand
- learn the student's chosen name and pronunciation
- identify which prior supports helped
- prepare materials, spaces, and routines
- begin the year with trust rather than surprise and correction

This is a Tier 1 prevent strategy: learn enough to remove foreseeable barriers while remaining open to what students and families teach later.

## Dignity, Consent, and Neurodiversity

Dignity means students are known as individuals with strengths and relationships, not just needs on a roster. Consent means information gathering is purposeful, respectful, and not a demand for unnecessary disclosure. Neurodiversity means movement, sensory access, communication, language, and bodymind variation are ordinary planning conditions.

## Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS

The Puzzle Plan links records, family knowledge, student work, physical space, language, and direct observation instead of allowing one test score or label to dominate. EQUITAS asks whether the family can access communication, whether the room assumes one body and language, and whether poverty, race, disability, and culture change who is understood or punished.
