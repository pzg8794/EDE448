Support is not positive because adults chose it. It is positive when the learner gains communication, access, agency, safety, and belonging.\\
{\color{PrimaryRed}\rule{4.4in}{0.8pt}}
\end{minipage}

\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}{0.95\textwidth}
\small
This journal uses my Puzzle Plan, an interdisciplinary framework that connects lived experience, education, data, and research so that no single diagnosis, behavior, observation, or score is mistaken for the whole learner. EQUITAS is the equity-centered heart of that framework. It asks whose knowledge is included, what context is missing, how power and bias shape interpretation, and whether a decision expands access, dignity, agency, and belonging. Applied to Module 2, these commitments make support an evidence-and-justice problem: adults must examine not only whether behavior changes, but also whether the learner gains meaningful influence over the goal, method, communication route, and definition of success.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}
