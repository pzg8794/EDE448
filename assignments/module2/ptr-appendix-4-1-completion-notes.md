## Tentative Functional Hypothesis

When an adult abruptly attempted to remove a familiar laptop and initiate a transition to a less familiar hands-on task, the learner protested and retained the device. The behavior may have helped delay the abrupt transition and preserve access to a familiar tool that supported predictability, regulation, confidence, or communication. When the environment allowed the laptop and blocks to coexist, the learner joined the activity.

This hypothesis is deliberately tentative. A valid PTR process would require the learner's perspective when accessible, team and family knowledge, repeated observations across routines, and data about what happens before and after the behavior.

## Draft Prevent-Teach-Reinforce Response

### Prevent

- preview the transition with a visible first/next sequence
- show the hands-on task before asking for a switch
- offer a short overlap period in which both tools remain available
- provide choices such as laptop beside blocks, laptop timer, partner start, or observe-first
- avoid removing a regulating or familiar item without first understanding its role
- give one direction at a time and make the entry step visible

### Teach

- model several ways to communicate "not yet," "more time," "keep this with me," "help," "break," and "show me"
- provide verbal, visual, gestural, AAC, pointing, and partner-supported communication routes
- practice transition choices outside a pressured moment
- teach adults to recognize protest as information and respond consistently

### Reinforce

- honor the communication route whenever safely possible
- recognize beginning, re-entering, self-advocating, asking for support, and participating through an alternate route
- use natural reinforcement: continued access, successful entry, meaningful contribution, and increased control over the transition
- do not make quietness, eye contact, speed, or surrendering the laptop the success criterion

## Data Needed Before Calling This a Plan

- the learner's own communication about the laptop and transition
- whether the pattern occurs across other transitions
- what supports are already in the IEP or classroom routine
- latency to begin under different transition supports
- communication route used
- whether the learner participates, returns, or remains connected
- adult implementation fidelity
- family and team knowledge about familiar tools, regulation, communication, and prior successful transitions
- whether the support increases dignity, agency, belonging, safety, and access rather than only reducing visible protest

## EQUITAS Audit

The Puzzle Plan is Piter's interdisciplinary framework for combining lived experience, education, data, and research to understand complex problems more fully. EQUITAS is the equity-focused lens within that framework; it asks whose knowledge is included, what context is missing, and where power or bias may shape decisions.

Applied here, that means:

- the learner's protest is one piece of evidence, not a complete explanation
- keeping a familiar tool may be an access support rather than a reward for "bad behavior"
- the adult's transition design belongs in the data
- communication modes must not be ranked by how typical they appear
- the learner should not have to surrender regulation or identity in order to be counted as participating
