# AAC for Children and Youth With Autism: Evidence and Application

## Source

This textbook chapter by Schlosser, Sigafoos, Shane, Koul, and Raghavendra reviews augmentative and alternative communication for children and youth with autism spectrum disorder.

## Main Ideas

The chapter treats AAC as support for both expressive communication and comprehension through aided and unaided means. It reviews assessment and feature matching, aided input, picture exchange, speech-generating devices, symbol representation, and strategies for evaluating the strength and relevance of intervention evidence.

A useful implication is that AAC should not be limited to requesting. A student needs vocabulary and opportunities for social interaction, information exchange, academic participation, and language growth. The chapter also reminds readers to separate the communication method from the instructional practice around it: a symbol, app, or device cannot be evaluated apart from modeling, prompting, partners, opportunities, and generalization.

## Critical Reading

Evidence varies by outcome and study design. A learner acquiring prompted requests in a controlled activity does not demonstrate spontaneous, flexible communication across relationships and settings. Intervention research must be read for participant characteristics, message functions, comparison conditions, maintenance, generalization, and social validity. Autism-specific findings should not be treated as universal feature-matching rules.

## Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS

The source supports the Puzzle Plan's insistence that no single outcome stand alone. EQUITAS asks whether research measures adult-desired performance or increased authorship, whether the vocabulary reflects the student's identities and communities, and whether comprehension support is available before adults judge behavior or competence.

## Assignment Use

- Broad evidence background for the AAC Review.
- Reminder that the Communication Support Plan must include commenting, questions, refusal, repair, social language, and academic language.

## Takeaway

AAC evidence is strongest when it shows meaningful communication expanding across functions, partners, and settings.
