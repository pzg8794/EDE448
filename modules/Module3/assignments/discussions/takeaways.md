# Module 3: Takeaways

## Blackboard Prompt

Which readings did you find useful in this module? What information will you consider in your future practice?

## Review Draft - Not Posted

The three readings I found most useful work together because they move communication support from the device, to the classroom, to the meaning behind a student's actions. Norrie, Waller, and Hannah (2021) showed that high-tech AAC adoption can fail when training, technical support, staffing, device fit, and school infrastructure are weak. Their study reinforced that a device placed near a student is not the same as communication access. Downing, Hanreddy, and Peckham-Hardin (2015) helped me see the general education classroom as a communicative environment that must be analyzed for actual opportunities across routines and peer interactions. Peckham-Hardin's (2015) chapter on communication and challenging behavior added that an alternative message works only when partners recognize and honor its function.

This connects to what I observed in my teaching placement and EDU486 camp. Participation expanded when language demands were reduced, technology steps were modeled, and youth could demonstrate, point, draw, write, speak, partner, pause, or return. Those experiences remind me that communication access is not one specialized moment. It is built into who may participate, how a response can be given, and whether the response changes what happens next.

In my future practice, I will treat AAC as a multimodal system rather than one device. I will keep primary and backup options available, provide vocabulary for refusal, commenting, questions, humor, academic ideas, and repair, and teach adults and peers to model, wait, confirm meaning, and respond. I will also redesign communication logs so they record the student's message, mode, environmental conditions, supports, barriers, successful repairs, and family knowledge rather than only adult judgments about behavior.

The Puzzle Plan is my interdisciplinary framework for bringing lived experience, education, data, research, technology, and advocacy together. EQUITAS is its equity-centered heart: it asks whose knowledge is included, what context is missing, and whether a decision increases dignity, agency, belonging, and access. Module 3 gives that framework a practical test. After communication support is provided, does the student have more authorship and more power to influence the classroom? If not, the system still needs to change.
