# Norrie, Waller, and Hannah (2021): Establishing Context for AAC

## Source

Christopher S. Norrie, Annalu Waller, and Elizabeth F. S. Hannah conducted a five-month mixed-methods ethnographic study in one special-education school. The article appears in *ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction* and examines high-tech AAC adoption and support.

## What the Study Found

The study exposes a large gap between AAC's technical promise and its use in school. Only six pupils among roughly 180 pupils with complex communication needs were active high-tech AAC users. Barriers included device fit and weight, brief or uneven training, lack of high-tech AAC training for classroom assistants, technical-support failures, staffing and recruitment constraints, policy gaps, and weak coordination among the communicator, educator, family, and technology.

Facilitators included motivated staff and families, multimodal communication, and support that responded to real classroom use. The authors propose an Assistive Technologist role to connect technical, pedagogical, and communication expertise.

## Critical Reading

The five-month embedded design provides richer contextual evidence than a one-time survey, but the study is still one school with limited adult diversity and substantial interpretation by one primary researcher. It establishes implementation problems and design needs; it does not prove that one staffing model will solve them everywhere.

## Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS

The Puzzle Plan treats the device as one piece rather than the whole picture. EQUITAS asks who is blamed when an AAC system is heavy, poorly positioned, unsupported, unavailable with assistants, or disconnected from instruction. Norrie et al. move responsibility toward systems and communication partners. The fair question is not “Why did the student fail to use AAC?” but “Which conditions made communication possible or impossible?”

This directly matches the teaching-placement pattern in which participation changed after language demands and technology steps were redesigned.
