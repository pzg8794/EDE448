# Module 3 Framework and Cross-Course Evidence

## Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS

The **Puzzle Plan** is Piter Garcia's interdisciplinary framework for bringing lived experience, education, data, research, technology, and advocacy together rather than allowing one label or data point to define a person. **EQUITAS** is the equity-centered heart of that framework: it asks whose knowledge is included, what context is missing, how power and bias shape interpretation, and whether a support actually increases dignity, agency, belonging, and access.

For Module 3, this becomes an evidence question: a student's competence cannot be measured fairly when the available communication modes are inaccessible or when adults ignore refusals, pauses, gestures, partner-supported responses, or device-generated language.

## Fresh EDE448 Work

- *From Compliance to Communication* showed that allowing a familiar laptop to coexist with hands-on materials removed an unnecessary either-or condition and changed participation. Module 3 adds the communication question: how can the learner ask to keep the tool, request a different sequence, or repair the adult's interpretation?
- *Words That Have Evidence* argued that positive self-talk should be grounded in truthful evidence and that failure can be reframed as learning. Module 3 adds accessible ways to author that evidence through symbols, speech, writing, images, selection, or a partner-supported message.
- *Who Has More Power After Support?* made power the test of dignity. Module 3 operationalizes that test: after support, can the student choose, refuse, comment, ask, repair, and influence what happens next?

## EDU486 Camp Evidence

The camp did not rely on one participation form. Youth could look, move, choose, count, use tools, photograph, draw, listen, explain, question, partner, pause, and return. Identity Beads represented complementary contributions rather than ranking identities. During evidence-to-action work, youth could revise models with labels and arrows, vote or pass, create a wearable message with words or symbols, and teach at the showcase by demonstrating, pointing, answering, asking, holding evidence, partnering, or observing.

These documented routes do not prove AAC use by a particular camper. They do provide an authentic design base for a prospective communication plan: communication opportunities should be distributed across the activity, not reserved for public speech.

## Teaching Placement Evidence

The Pine Brook technology placement showed participation changing when directions were clarified, technology steps were modeled, language demands were reduced, and students could use familiar tools alongside new materials. The implication for AAC is that implementation belongs to the environment and communication partners, not only to the student.

## Module 3 Design Commitments

- Keep primary and low-tech backup communication available across routines.
- Offer vocabulary for commenting, refusing, questioning, joking, repairing, and academic participation, not only requesting.
- Train adults and peers to model, wait, notice, confirm, and act without demanding performance.
- Document modes, conditions, barriers, successful repairs, and student messages rather than producing a compliance score.
- Treat family knowledge, student preference, and response to support as evidence.
