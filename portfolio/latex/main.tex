\section*{Assignment and Design Standard}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Assignment and Design Standard}

This culminating portfolio is worth 40 percent of the EDE448 grade and is due August 7, 2026. The Blackboard prompt contains the internally inconsistent phrase ``at least ten (8) resources/supports.'' I use the more demanding interpretation: ten resources, each designed to stand alone within one or two pages. Every entry explains the support for a teacher or family audience, connects it to communication, social-emotional learning, or behavior, identifies when and why it should be used, and provides relevant sources or websites.

The portfolio reworks applicable Module 1, 2, and 3 assignments into practitioner tools rather than stacking ten old papers together. Its evidence core is a communication system already built through EDU486: a 30-second student video memo after an activity, an every-day personalized souvenir, a day recap evidence story, and a full-week personalized family recap organized per attended day and per activity. Six connected EDE448 resources extend the same commitments to sensory access, peer culture, PTR, AAC, backup communication, and flexible roles. Copyrighted course packets and private student records are not reproduced.

\toolsection{Evidence Boundary}

The Pine Brook technology-placement and EDU486 camp examples are de-identified practice records, not diagnoses or universal student traits. The four artifact resources describe completed private production files without reproducing participant faces, names, family contacts, personalized stories, or parent messages. Plans do not prove implementation; photographs show action but not complete understanding; automatic transcripts require source review before direct quotation. Day 5 has photographs and a same-session clean transcript but no source video. The full-week family packages were completed and prepared for delivery, but no delivery receipt or family response was recovered. The Communication Support Plan remains prospective and never claims that a particular camper used AAC; the Kit for Kids evidence remains limited to de-identified teacher notes and two anonymous responses.

\section*{Portfolio-Wide Reflection and Revision Commitments}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Portfolio-Wide Reflection and Revision Commitments}

Across these ten resources, the most important change in my thinking is that support cannot be judged by whether behavior becomes quieter, work becomes faster, or adults experience fewer disruptions. The camp communication system shows a concrete alternative. The video memo gives youth an immediate account after an activity; the souvenir recognizes an individual contribution that day; the day recap reconstructs every activity for collective memory and next-day planning; and the family recap carries every attended day and its activity sequence into a full-week story. These are distinct feedback timescales, not four versions of the same form.

The teaching placement first taught me to make routes visible, separate access from rigor, and notice communication in action, artifacts, questions, and multimodal responses. The Puzzle Plan carried those lessons into camp by joining student voice with scientific evidence, context, lesson design, and adult decisions while refusing to let one observation define a learner. EQUITAS added the power test: whose account survives, who controls the record, and whether a message can change materials, pacing, roles, the shared model, institutional understanding, or family communication. A polished artifact is not evidence of agency if youth cannot correct, restrict, or alter it.

The evidence boundaries keep that system accountable. A photograph does not establish understanding; a plan does not prove implementation; an automatic transcript does not authorize an unchecked quotation; a family draft does not prove delivery; and absence of a reply does not show lack of care. In future practice, I will review these tools with students, families, educators, speech-language pathologists, paraprofessionals, and program partners across languages and communication modes. The strongest outcome is not more documentation. It is more student-authored evidence that changes adult design.
