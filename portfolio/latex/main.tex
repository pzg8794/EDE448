\section*{Assignment and Design Standard}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Assignment and Design Standard}

This culminating portfolio is worth 40 percent of the EDE448 grade and is due August 7, 2026. The Blackboard prompt contains the internally inconsistent phrase ``at least ten (8) resources/supports.'' I use the more demanding interpretation: ten resources, each designed to stand alone within one or two pages. Every entry explains the support for a teacher or family audience, connects it to communication, social-emotional learning, or behavior, identifies when and why it should be used, and provides relevant sources or websites. The ten resources occupy 20 pages; the title, assignment standard, contents, portfolio map, closing reflection, and references are six framing pages and are not counted toward the resource limit. The complete PDF therefore contains 26 physical pages.

The portfolio reworks applicable Module 1, 2, and 3 assignments into practitioner tools rather than stacking ten old papers together. Its evidence core is a communication system already built through EDU486: a 30-second student video memo after an activity, an every-day personalized souvenir, a day recap evidence story, and a full-week personalized family recap organized per attended day and per activity. Six connected EDE448 resources extend the same commitments to sensory access, peer culture, PTR, AAC, backup communication, and flexible roles. Three completed artifact pages are reproduced under signed parent/guardian consent for this internal UofR educational review; family contacts, private correspondence, and unrelated records are not reproduced.

\toolsection{Evidence Boundary}

The Pine Brook technology-placement examples remain de-identified practice records, not diagnoses or universal student traits. Resources 5, 7, and 9 reproduce actual completed EDU486 pages, including participant names and photographs, under signed parent/guardian consent for this internal UofR educational submission. Plans do not prove implementation; photographs show action but not complete understanding; automatic transcripts require source review before direct quotation. Day 5 has photographs and a same-session clean transcript but no source video. The full-week family packages were completed and prepared for delivery, but no delivery receipt or family response was recovered. The Communication Support Plan remains prospective and never claims that a particular camper used AAC; the Kit for Kids evidence remains limited to de-identified teacher notes and two anonymous responses.

\toolsection{From Teaching Placement to Course Practice}

Across this portfolio, my central learning is that inclusion is not placement, a device, or quieter behavior. It is built when sensory conditions, communication routes, peer norms, roles, and adult responses let learners understand, contribute, refuse, repair, and influence what happens next. Pine Brook made this concrete: competence in technology-rich lessons could be hidden by login friction, rapid directions, noise, crowded materials, unclear roles, peer pacing, or removal of a familiar tool. When one learner kept a laptop beside blocks, participation increased after adults changed access. The sensory walk, Kit for Kids lesson, and PTR inquiry taught me to examine the environment, separate observation from inference, teach peers to help without taking over, and redesign transitions before demanding tolerance \citep{guggenheimSensory,michigan2025sensory,oar2026kit,dunlap2019ptr}.

The EDE448 readings extended that field lesson: sensory access should be proactive; peer culture is part of positive support; AAC is multimodal rather than a last resort; and competence grows through opportunity, responsive partners, and consequential messages \citep{ashaAAC,downing2015classroom,jorgensen2018competence,peckhamhardin2015behavior}. Camp tested those ideas. Youth could point, tell, show, partner, observe, pass, return, change roles, use movement as a reset, and select public or private showcase participation while remaining part of rigorous environmental-science inquiry. Flexible routes did not lower expectations; they separated the intellectual goal from one required pace, posture, sensory tolerance, or performance mode.

\toolsection{What Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS Produced}

The \textbf{Puzzle Plan} turned these lessons into a connected evidence-to-design cycle. It places lived experience, student words, observable action, work products, scientific records, context, family knowledge, and adult decisions beside one another so no diagnosis, photograph, behavior count, or polished artifact becomes the whole story. Its camp deliverables work at four timescales: the video memo preserves an immediate account; the daily souvenir recognizes a contribution; the day recap reconstructs the sequence and changes next-day planning; and the full-week family recap carries a student-reviewed story across settings. The ten resources extend that cycle into sensory redesign, peer instruction, PTR, AAC and backup communication, family partnership, and flexible roles. Each enacts the framework's demand that evidence produce a teaching decision \citep{campEvidence2026}.

\textbf{EQUITAS}, the equity-centered heart of the Puzzle Plan, asks whose knowledge enters the record, whose contribution disappears, what context is missing, how power shapes interpretation, and who can revise the story. That test produced concrete safeguards: multiple communication modes, wait time, pass-and-return routes, consent to record or withhold, bilingual and low-tech options, changeable roles, separation of observation from interpretation, and evidence that a message changed materials, pacing, access, or an adult decision. If support reduces visible disruption while also reducing communication, trust, dignity, agency, or belonging, EQUITAS identifies a design failure rather than success.

\toolsection{Families, Community, and Revision Commitments}

This learning widened the unit of inclusion. Parents and families are not recipients at the end of a school-controlled pipeline. In the family recap, the learner controls what travels home and families can return language, context, priorities, and interpretations; family knowledge is evidence, not an afterthought \citep{zacarian2015community}. Peers share responsibility through invite, wait, ask, and respect; educators, support professionals, collaborators, and program leaders share responsibility for access and implementation. Community-facing science, policy roles, and showcase choices connect learning beyond the classroom while preserving a learner's right not to perform publicly. Inclusion redistributes responsibility and interpretive power across the learning community instead of training one child to adapt to every condition.

My revision commitment is to co-design these tools across languages and communication modes; track adult implementation and whether student messages produce change; offer accessible, translated, no-photo, no-message, and private options; invite family and community response without burden; and distinguish what was planned, prepared, delivered, received, and learned. The evidence boundaries remain: a photograph does not prove understanding, one event does not establish function, a transcript requires review, and a prepared family package does not prove receipt. The teacher's work is not to complete the puzzle for another person. Puzzle Plan keeps the pieces connected; EQUITAS asks who has power over their meaning. Together, they require support to leave learners and communities with more authorship, access, and power to shape what happens next.
