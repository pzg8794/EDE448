\toolsection{What This Resource Is}

A low-tech backup is a printed or durable communication display that preserves access when a speech-generating device is charging, unavailable, difficult to position, unsafe near materials, or not preferred in that moment. It is not a lesser system or a test before ``earning'' technology. Multimodal AAC combines aided and unaided methods and should change with the communicator's needs and contexts \citep{ashaAAC,mirenda2015aids}.

The starter below organizes vocabulary by function. It is a planning model, not a universal board. The communicator and speech-language pathologist should choose representation, language, size, layout, symbol system, color, spacing, contrast, and access method. Keep locations consistent with the primary system when that supports motor planning.

\toolsection{Build and Use Checklist}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Communicator first:} Confirm current modes, languages, vocabulary priorities, visual/literacy access, and whether a printed board is wanted.
  \item \textbf{Match access:} Choose direct point, eye pointing, partner-assisted scanning, switch-compatible scanning sequence, or another reliable route.
  \item \textbf{Protect message range:} Include refusal, pain/sensory messages, questions, comments, humor, academic language, and repair, not only requests.
  \item \textbf{Place copies intentionally:} Mount or store them where communication occurs: desk, lanyard, activity bin, transport, home, and emergency kit as appropriate.
  \item \textbf{Model without requiring repetition:} Partners point to words while speaking, then accept the communicator's response through any reliable mode.
  \item \textbf{Audit weekly:} Record missing words, access failures, damaged or absent boards, and whether messages affected what happened.
\end{enumerate}

\toolsection{Continuity Plan}

Name who prints and updates the board, who checks availability, where backups live, and how partners will respond during device failure. The primary and backup systems should share important vocabulary and predictable locations, but a learner should not be forced to use the paper display when gesture, writing, an object, or another mode is clearer. Implementation reliability is a system responsibility \citep{norrie2021context}.

\reflectionbox{Hands-on learning can make a device difficult to carry or position at exactly the moment a learner needs to reject a texture, direct a partner, comment on evidence, or repair a misunderstanding. The backup removes the false choice between participating with materials and retaining communication access. It also makes the team's preparedness visible: ``the device was unavailable'' should not become ``the student had nothing to say.''}

\adaptationbox{A dense grid may be inaccessible because of vision, motor, literacy, language, or attention needs. Reduce field size, increase spacing/contrast, use objects or photographs, add partner-assisted scanning, or create layered boards while preserving robust message functions. Do not change symbol locations casually. Any redesign should be trialed with the communicator, and rejection of the board is data rather than noncompliance.}
