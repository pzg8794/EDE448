\toolsection{The Existing Camp Artifact}

The camp team completed one reviewed recap presentation for each documented camp day, Days 1--5. These were not decorative photo slideshows. Each deck reconstructed the day's activity sequence, connected youth actions and words to the shared scientific model, preserved evidence limits, and made the next question visible. The recap let youth revisit the work, helped instructors redesign the next day, and gave collaborators and families a defensible account of what happened \citep{campEvidence2026}.

This is communication and positive behavioral support because it resists adult shorthand such as ``engaged'' or ``off task.'' It shows the route, role, evidence, questions, boundaries, and changes around participation. A learner can point, tell, show, partner, pass, and return while the rigorous group story remains available. Communication becomes reliable when student messages alter the shared account and what happens next \citep{peckhamhardin2015behavior}.

\toolsection{Build the Recap Before Planning Tomorrow}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Inventory every activity, including changes:} record what ran, changed order, shortened, moved, or did not happen.
  \item \textbf{Triangulate:} use artifacts, photographs, video/transcript records, student memos, measurements, and instructor notes according to their actual strength.
  \item \textbf{Preserve youth meaning:} identify direct words, questions, choices, dislikes, model changes, and contributions; do not infer understanding from a face or photograph.
  \item \textbf{Rebuild the storyline:} show what the group observed, how the explanation changed, what remained unknown, and what should happen next.
  \item \textbf{Keep access visible:} repeat the available recall routes and represent passing, role changes, movement, sensory boundaries, and re-entry as part of learning.
  \item \textbf{Review and circulate by audience:} verify identifiers, attribution, claims, consent, and the version each approved audience is permitted to receive.
\end{enumerate}

\toolsection{Evidence That Changed the Story}

The Day 2 recap names the racket game as a movement reset, teamwork, joy, and re-entry route rather than lost instructional time. Day 3 repeats ``point, tell, show, partner, pass + return'' on every activity slide. Day 4 records a sand check as not completed and refuses to claim a result. The later youth retrospective asks for less rushing, more investigation time, physical activity, and numbered showcase stations. The evidence system therefore changes both interpretation and design.

\toolsection{Puzzle Plan and EQUITAS in Practice}

The Puzzle Plan joins student voice with artifacts, context, scientific evidence, and adult decisions while keeping contradictions visible. EQUITAS asks whose words enter the recap, who is shown only as a body in a photograph, whose refusal or uncertainty disappears, and who gets to revise the institutional story. Voice counts as influence only when it can alter tomorrow's lesson, the team's explanation, what leaders understand, or what families receive.

\reflectionbox{\textbf{Artifact proof:} Five reviewed decks survive for Days 1--5. Day 6 has a clean retrospective transcript but no sixth deck. Day 5 has photographs and a same-session transcript but no source video; its recap limits what photographs establish \citep{campEvidence2026}.}

\adaptationbox{Recaps can privilege the most verbal, photographed, or adult-approved story. Invite correction through speech, AAC, writing, drawing, pointing, home language, or private review. Keep disagreement and ``not yet'' visible; never circulate identifiable records beyond consented readers.}
