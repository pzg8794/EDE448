\toolsection{What This Resource Is}

This tool structures group work so students can contribute without one pace, posture, communication mode, sensory tolerance, or public role becoming the price of belonging. It combines the Kit for Kids peer actions---invite, wait, ask, and respect---with visible, changeable roles and explicit permission to pass or return \citep{oar2026kit,oar2026tips}. The goal is not to assign a ``safe'' minor role permanently. It is to make the intellectual and social work of the group accessible while preserving choice.

Peer interaction is a rich communication context when students can initiate, direct, disagree, ask, repair, and influence shared work \citep{downing2015classroom}. Group systems should therefore teach peers how to support without taking over and teach adults to redesign noise, timing, role ambiguity, material competition, or oral-response bias.

\toolsection{Group Start Script}

Display the goal, materials, estimated time, and role menu. Then say:

\begin{quote}
Choose a way to begin. You may work in a role, combine roles, ask for a different one, observe first, or pass and return. Ask before helping. Wait for a response. Keep communication tools with their owner. A good group changes its plan when someone identifies an access barrier.
\end{quote}

Give each student a way to communicate: \textit{my turn, your turn, wait, help, show me, not yet, stop, different role, I disagree, I have an idea, you misunderstood,} and \textit{I am ready to return}. Use a visible turn sequence only when helpful; do not require every student to contribute in the same order or volume.

\toolsection{Review With Students}

Ask which roles were real, whose ideas changed the product, whether anyone had to perform a preferred social style to participate, and what barrier adults should redesign. Record contribution in multiple forms rather than ranking visible leadership over quiet evidence work. Constructing competence requires environments that expect contribution while supplying access, not assumptions that a learner is incapable because the usual route failed \citep{jorgensen2018competence}.

\reflectionbox{EDU486 activities documented complementary contributions through tool use, labels, arrows, photography, demonstration, observation, partnering, model revision, and showcase roles \citep{campEvidence2026}. The Identity Beads activity reinforced that different contributions can coexist without becoming a hierarchy. The design lesson is to make roles visible and flexible while keeping the shared intellectual goal intact.}

\adaptationbox{Peer structures cannot repair inaccessible instruction by themselves. Adults must address noise, pace, material layout, language, conflict, and status. Do not assign one student as a permanent helper or one disabled student as a permanent observer. Revisit roles across activities, support private and bilingual communication, and treat refusal or a request for individual work as information.}
