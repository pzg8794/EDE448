\toolsection{What This Resource Is}

This record replaces adult-centered ratings with a two-way account of communication, access conditions, barriers, and student-authored meaning. The Daily Communication Logs packet includes useful home--school structures, but several examples emphasize good/fair/poor days, warnings, sitting, compliance, or initials \citep{dailylogs2009}. Those fields can make the student an object of reporting and hide whether the environment, support, or adult response contributed to the day.

The redesigned record supports communication and behavior by preserving what the student chose, refused, asked, commented, or repaired; how the message was expressed; which support increased access; and what barrier should change. Family context travels back to school as evidence rather than as an afterthought. The learner reviews, changes, or declines the outgoing message.

\toolsection{Implementation and Consent Protocol}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Agree with the student and family on purpose, language, frequency, privacy, and who may read the record.
  \item Offer speech, AAC, writing, typing, drawing, photographs, objects, partner-assisted selection, or no message.
  \item Describe observable conditions and supports; do not infer motives or assign moral ratings.
  \item Let the student select, edit, approve, or withhold information before it travels.
  \item Invite family response in the family's language and preferred format; absence of a response is not lack of care.
  \item Review patterns after five school days and make a joint keep/change/stop decision after ten.
\end{enumerate}

\toolsection{What to Review}

Look across entries for message functions, modes, access conditions, partner wait/modeling, breakdowns and repairs, whether AAC/backup was available, and whether communication changed the environment. Do not use the record to count ``good days'' or establish a one-way compliance score. Ask whether the same barrier recurs, whether adults implement agreed supports, and whether the student experiences greater authorship across home and school.

Family knowledge should not be treated as secondary to school data. Classroom-community planning requires attention to language, accommodation, strengths, relationships, and the family's understanding of successful support \citep{zacarian2015community}. At the same time, families differ in time, technology access, trust, and capacity. Offer paper, digital, phone, translated, and less-frequent options without penalty.

\reflectionbox{The strongest shift is from ``How was the student today?'' to ``What did the student communicate, under what conditions, what changed because of it, and what should adults redesign?'' That shift makes the daily record useful to the learner rather than merely useful about the learner. It also protects against a school narrative becoming the only evidence that travels home.}

\adaptationbox{Daily reporting can become surveillance, expose private health/disability information, or create family burden. Collect only information needed for support; follow school privacy policy; avoid public or unsecured channels; and honor the student's right not to share. Some learners may prefer a weekly summary, event-triggered message, photo choice, or no formal log. The form changes when consent or usefulness changes.}
